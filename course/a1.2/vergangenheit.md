# Vergangenheit — the complete system

Every past-tense thing the course teaches, each with an example. A1.2 Units 1, 6 and 7 —
the Perfekt is introduced in U6, Präteritum *sein/haben* in U1, *denken/wissen/es gibt* in
U7. A1.1 has no past tense.

Form inventory for the participles: [`partizip-ii.md`](partizip-ii.md).
Source: `courses/a1.2/` booklet + LÖSUNGEN. Nothing here is invented.

**The one line that covers the whole thing:** Perfekt is *assembled* (auxiliary +
participle), Präteritum is *conjugated* (one form). That's why spoken German uses Perfekt
— one participle per verb beats six conjugated forms.

---

## A. The two past forms

| # | Thing | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Perfekt** — 2+ words, the spoken past | *Ich **habe** gefrühstückt.* |
| 2 | **Präteritum** — 1 word, the written past | *Ich **war** zu Hause.* |

One past *time*, two forms. Which one you use is **register, not meaning** — see [D](#d-choosing-between-them).

## B. Perfekt = auxiliary + Partizip II

### B1 — which auxiliary

| # | Thing | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | **haben** — the default | *Was **hast** du gestern gemacht?* |
| 4 | **sein** — Ortswechsel (A → B) | *Sie **ist** zur S-Bahn gelaufen.* |
| 5 | **sein** — Zustandsänderung | *Er **ist** spät eingeschlafen.* |
| 6 | **sein** — *passieren, bleiben, werden* | *Was **ist** passiert?* · *Ich **bin** zu Hause geblieben.* · *Katie **ist** Friseurin geworden.* |
| 7 | **sein** — *sein* itself, participle *gewesen* | *Wie **ist** dein Tag gewesen?* |

The auxiliary follows the **meaning** of the verb, not whether it's irregular: *laufen →
ist gelaufen* (strong) but *schlafen → hat geschlafen* (also strong).

Same stem, opposite auxiliary: *stehen → **hat** gestanden* vs *aufstehen → **ist**
aufgestanden*; *schlafen → **hat** geschlafen* vs *einschlafen → **ist** eingeschlafen*.

### B2 — which participle

Nine groups, all listed in [`partizip-ii.md`](partizip-ii.md):

| # | Group | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 8 | regular — `ge + Stamm + t` | *Ich habe das Perfekt **gelernt**.* |
| 9 | regular — `ge + Stamm + et` (stem -d/-t/-m/-n) | *Ich habe gestern **gearbeitet**.* |
| 10 | **-ieren** — no *ge-* | *Wir haben mit Katie **telefoniert**.* |
| 11 | separable — *ge* inside | *Ich habe das Buch **mitgenommen**.* |
| 12 | irregular — `ge…en` | *Wir haben einen Kuchen **gegessen**.* |
| 13 | irregular — `ge…t` | *Bobbi hat das nicht **gewusst**.* |
| 14 | no *ge-* / special | *gewesen · gehabt · geworden · passiert* |

### B3 — where the parts go

| # | Thing | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 15 | statement: auxiliary **position 2**, participle **at the end** | *Ich habe die ganze Nacht **durchtrainiert**.* |
| 16 | W-question: auxiliary stays position 2 | *Was **hast** du gestern **gemacht**?* |
| 17 | Ja/Nein-question: auxiliary to position 1 | ***Hast** du gestern Hausaufgaben **gemacht**?* |

## C. Präteritum

| # | Thing | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 18 | ***sein* → war** · ***haben* → hatte** — *ich* and *er/sie/es* are the same form; use these in speech, not the Perfekt | *Wo **warst** du gestern?* · *Ich **hatte** eine kleine Wohnung.* |
| 19 | the set that prefers Präteritum in speech | *Ich **dachte**, du kommst morgen.* · *Ich **wusste** die Antwort nicht.* · *Es **gab** keine Milch.* |

*Es gab* is the Präteritum of *es gibt* — one word, no auxiliary, no *ge-*.

### What *war* and *hatte* actually mean

| Form | English | Verb | Partizip II — don't confuse |
|---|---|---|---|
| *war* | was / were | *sein* — to be | *gewesen* |
| *hatte* | had | *haben* — to have | *gehabt* |

English does the same thing with the same two verbs: *I am → I **was*** · *I have → I
**had***.

*war* and *hatte* are Präteritum, **not** participles. The participle only ever appears
inside a Perfekt (*ist gewesen*, *hat gehabt*). For the plain past of *sein*/*haben*,
German prefers the Präteritum: **war**, **hatte**.

**Watch the verb, not the English.** *Welche Farbe **hatte** dein Fahrrad?* = “What color
was your bike?” — German says the bike **has** a color, so it's *hatte*, not *war*. (U9
confirms in the present: *Welche Farbe **hat** das Kleid?*.) *Wie **war** dein Fahrrad?*
would ask what it was *like*, not what color it was.

Each has only **four** distinct forms, not six: *ich* = *er/sie/es*, and *wir* = *sie/Sie*.

### The booklet prints its own proof

Eight pairs from the U6/U7 answer keys — Perfekt on the left, Präteritum on the right, both
given as equivalents:

| Perfekt (contains the participle) | = | Präteritum (what you say) |
|---|---|---|
| *Wie ist dein Tag **gewesen**?* | = | *Wie **war** dein Tag?* |
| *Ich bin fünf Minuten zu spät **gewesen**.* | = | *Ich **war** fünf Minuten zu spät.* |
| *Ich bin in Australien **gewesen**.* | = | *Ich **war** in Australien.* |
| *Wir sind am Alexanderplatz **gewesen**.* | = | *Wir **waren** am Alexanderplatz.* |
| *Ich habe heute frei **gehabt**.* | = | *Ich **hatte** heute frei.* |
| *Er hat keinen Hunger **gehabt**.* | = | *Er **hatte** keinen Hunger.* |
| *Ihr habt einen Tisch **gehabt**.* | = | *Ihr **hattet** einen Tisch.* |
| *Katie hat eine Wohnung **gehabt**.* | = | *Katie **hatte** eine kleine Wohnung.* |

*gewesen* and *gehabt* **never appear alone** — always inside a Perfekt with *bin/bist/ist*
or *habe/hast/hat*. That part is absolute.

But **“preferred” is not “never used.”** The swap above only works for the plain past of
*sein*/*haben*. Outside that, *gewesen* is required and *war* can't replace it — the course
itself has one, in an A1.1 U9 dialog:

| | |
|---|---|
| *Das wär' doch nicht nötig **gewesen**.* | You didn't have to do that. (A1.1 U9) |

That's *wäre* + *gewesen* — *would have been*. *Das war nicht nötig* says something else
(it happened and wasn't necessary), so there is no *war* version. *gehabt* shows up in
speech the same way, in fixed idioms like *Schwein **gehabt**!* and *Glück **gehabt**!*

### The five, and what you'd otherwise say

It isn't a *situation* that decides — it's the **verb**. For these five, the Perfekt is
replaced wholesale:

| Verb | You'd otherwise say | You say |
|---|---|---|
| *sein* | ~~*Ich bin zu Hause gewesen.*~~ | ***Ich war** zu Hause.* |
| *haben* | ~~*Ich habe keine Zeit gehabt.*~~ | ***Ich hatte** keine Zeit.* |
| *denken* | ~~*Ich habe gedacht …*~~ | ***Ich dachte** …* |
| *wissen* | ~~*Ich habe gewusst …*~~ | ***Ich wusste** …* |
| *es gibt* | ~~*Es hat Milch gegeben.*~~ | ***Es gab** Milch.* |

The Perfekt versions are grammatical — the booklet says so — but it also says *wir
bevorzugen das Präteritum*. So they're **used**, just not the default. Everything else in
the past, when speaking, is Perfekt.

### The forms you produce

| | sein | haben |
|---|---|---|
| ich | **war** | **hatte** |
| du | warst | hattest |
| er/sie/es | **war** | **hatte** |
| wir | **waren** | hatten |
| ihr | wart | hattet |
| sie/Sie | **waren** | hatten |

*ich* = *er/sie/es* (no ending — unlike Präsens *ich habe* / *er hat*). *wir* = *sie/Sie*.

Everything else in the Präteritum is **recognition only** at this level — you meet it in
stories and news. The regular pattern (stem + *-te*) exists; the booklet defers it.

## D. Choosing between them

| | |
|---|---|
| **Register decides, not meaning** | *Wie **war** dein Tag?* = *Wie **ist** dein Tag gewesen?* |
| Speaking → Perfekt | *Ich habe gekocht.* |
| Stories / news / literature → Präteritum | *Katie hatte eine kleine Wohnung.* |
| Speaking, but unavoidable → Präteritum | *sein, haben, denken, wissen, es gibt* |

**For you, now: default to Perfekt.** The only Präteritum you need to *produce* is five
words — *war · hatte · dachte · wusste · es gab*. General *ich kochte, ich ging* is
**recognition-only**; you'll read it in stories, you don't build it. Even the booklet's
own storytelling exercise uses the Perfekt: *Zuerst **hat** Gogo **gefrühstückt**. Dann
**hat** er seine Ex-Frau **abgeholt**.*

**Register** = the variety of language a situation calls for: spoken vs written, casual vs
formal. English does this with vocabulary — *kids* / *children* / *offspring* mean the same
thing at three registers. German does it with the past tense.

So don't hunt for a shade of meaning between *ich habe gekocht* and *ich kochte* — there
isn't one. Ask instead: **am I talking, or narrating?**
